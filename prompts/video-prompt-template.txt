=== HOW TO USE (delete this block before pasting) ===
1. Fill every {{...}} in <inputs>. Write "ask me" or "you decide" where unsure.
2. In <type>, keep ONE block (A, B, C or D) and delete the other three.
3. Open Claude Code (Opus 5.5 or stronger) in an empty folder and paste the prompt.
4. Values can be written in Chinese; on-screen text follows the language you set in <inputs>.
Sources of every rule: Project.md, section 8.
=== END ===

<role>
You are the director, motion designer, engineer and sound designer of one short
film. You make it by writing code that draws every frame, rendering it frame by
frame in a headless browser, and encoding it with ffmpeg. The bar is high-end
studio work. A clean, template-looking result is a fail.
</role>

<inputs>
Topic, product or story: {{TOPIC}}
The one thing the viewer must remember: {{TAKEAWAY}}
Audience and where they watch: {{AUDIENCE_AND_PLATFORM}}
Source material: {{SOURCE}}  (article, script, narration audio, product URL or repo path, reference video, or "none")
Facts that must appear exactly: {{MUST_INCLUDE}}
Duration: {{DURATION}} s
Aspect ratios: {{ASPECTS}}  (16:9 1920x1080 / 9:16 1080x1920 / 1:1 1080x1080)
Frame rate: {{FPS}}  (30 default; 24 for hand-drawn or cinematic looks; 60 for UI motion)
On-screen text language: {{ON_SCREEN_LANGUAGE}}
Style: {{STYLE}}  (a named style, a reference image or video path, a few mood words, or "propose three")
Brand colours and fonts: {{BRAND}}
Music: {{MUSIC}}  (my file path / synthesize in code / none)
Narration: {{NARRATION}}  (my audio path / TTS voice / none)
Subtitles: {{SUBTITLES}}  (burned in / .srt only / none)
Check-ins: {{CHECKINS}}  ("pick plan only" / "pick plan, then approve stills" / "none, run unattended")
Extra bans for this film: {{EXTRA_BANS}}
</inputs>

<process>
Work in ./film/<slug>/ with source/, previews/, brief.md, index.html, audio/, qa/, out/.
1. Environment. Check for Node or Python with Playwright and a Chromium, and an
   ffmpeg whose encoder list includes libx264. Tell me what is missing and how to
   install it; do not install system software without asking.
2. Questions. Ask me once, in one message, at most four questions about things
   that change the film: facts you cannot verify, missing material, the style if
   I wrote "propose three". Fill every other gap with a sensible default and
   write the default down. Do not come back with more questions later. With
   check-ins "none", ask nothing and record your assumptions in brief.md.
3. Three plans. They must differ in visual language or narrative structure, not
   only in colour: A stays closest to the material, B changes the visual
   language, C changes how it is told (one continuous shot, one recurring hero
   object, one metaphor). For each give a name, one sentence, the style with
   2-4 hex colours and fonts, the tech route, a shot list (time, what is on
   screen, how it moves, how it exits), the sound, the estimated render time and
   the main risk. Render one real key frame per plan with real text, colours and
   fonts, never placeholder boxes. Look at the three images yourself, fix obvious
   problems, then show them to me and wait for my pick. With check-ins "none",
   choose yourself and write why in the report.
4. brief.md. Write the chosen plan in full as a reusable prompt: inputs,
   direction, banned list, cue sheet or narration timeline, shot list, build
   rules, gotchas. Follow it from here on. If there is narration or music I
   supplied, prepare and measure it now, before any picture (see <sound>).
5. Build shot by shot. After each shot render three stills (entrance, main
   action, end) and look at them. Fix overflow, overlap, small subjects and
   unreadable text before starting the next shot. For films over 30 s, render a
   half-resolution preview without motion blur once every shot exists, check
   pace and transitions, then polish. With check-ins "approve stills", show me
   three stills (opening, middle, end) before the full render.
6. Once the picture is locked, synthesize music and effects on the same cue
   sheet, mix and master, render the full film, then follow <review>.
7. <deliver>.
</process>

<type>
--- A. Motion-design piece (brand ident, showreel, launch teaser) ---
- Usually 10-20 s, on a 120 BPM grid (one beat = 0.5 s). Something intentional happens on
  every beat; cuts land on beats or half-bars.
- Write the style's signature features as a checklist an expert could verify
  (for cel animation: on twos at 24 fps, one smear frame on fast moves, line
  boil every 2 frames, paper grain multiplied at 10-15%) and map each to a moment.
- A structure that reads as high-end: one shape that never cuts, morphing size,
  corner radius and colour between states while its content swaps with a short
  blur; a cursor drives every change; the last frame equals the first so it loops.
- Benchmarks: Buck, ManvsMachine, Apple keynote motion.

--- B. Narrated explainer (15-60 s of narration, or a script to voice) ---
- Get word-level timings: transcribe my audio and correct it against my script,
  or synthesize the script line by line and measure each line. Anchor every
  animation to a word or phrase, never to a hard-coded second, so a new
  recording re-times the whole film.
- Shots follow sentences, one idea per shot, 1.5-5 s each. For every shot write
  what it must make clear, 2-4 keywords, entrance, main action, secondary
  action and transition.
- Turn each abstract concept into a visible object that moves, and keep one
  metaphor per concept for the whole film: scope is a frame; too much is a pile
  that tips over; categories are cards with a bracket and a stamp; a process is
  numbered steps with arrows; growth is bars rising; forgetting is something
  falling out of frame and turning grey.
- Pick one explainer grammar and keep it: 3Blue1Brown (one object on black,
  transforms, colour = concept name), Kurzgesagt (the concept becomes an object,
  the camera travels between scales), Vox (an evidence desk, the camera moves
  like an investigator's eye, elements step at 12 fps), whiteboard (say a line,
  write a line), hand-drawn notes, kinetic type (the words are the actors), or a
  data chart (axes, then data, then one annotation, values honest).
- Give the two core nouns two fixed accent colours, used the same way in
  picture, data and subtitles.
- Subtitles: one line, about 20 CJK characters at most, the bottom 170 px kept
  for them (higher on 9:16 if the platform's buttons cover the bottom). Also export an .srt and a version without burned-in subtitles.
- Do not draw faces or people in code. Use hands, objects, or a character I
  provide.

--- C. Product promo from my real web app or front-end project ---
- Product: {{URL_OR_REPO}}. Read it first: name, one-line promise, three real
  capabilities, numbers only if the source states them, colours and fonts from
  its design system or computed styles.
- Story: a hook on the pain or the promise (2-3 beats), the name reveal, how it
  works in three beats with one concrete visual each, proof only if real, an end
  card with name, tagline and URL held 2 s. 20-40 s, beats of 2-5 s, never more
  than 8 words on screen.
- Capture the real UI with realistic fake data (never empty databases, lorem
  ipsum or real customer data): a full-page 2x screenshot, element cutouts with
  transparent backgrounds, an "empty plate" with each flying element hidden, a 4x
  shot of the hero element, and a layout.json of element boxes in page
  coordinates. Animate crops of these screenshots, never redraw UI text. Every
  element that flies in lands in its real slot.
- 2.5D camera: a perspective container with keyframed centre, zoom and
  rotation; use CSS zoom rather than scale inside it so text stays sharp.
  Parallax is one drive value times per-layer factors such as 0.35 / 0.7 / 1.4,
  with the move computed backwards so the hero ends centred.
- At most two crash zooms in the film; no handheld shake in a bright UI promo.
- Deliver a version with music and one without, from the same timeline.

--- D. Story or art-style short with characters ---
- Write TREATMENT.md first: logline, arc, a shot table (time | what is on screen
  | what it does | camera | on-screen text | how it is made) and a cue map. Every
  shot needs an event: something changes between its first and last frame. Gags
  must read: fast actions, slow meanings, one read at a time.
- Lock style, palette and characters before code. Write a style card with fixed
  sections: essence and what it is not; materials and rendering; colour logic;
  type; motion quality; camera grammar; sound palette; native moves; pitfalls.
  Render a character model sheet with the real drawing code and show it to me.
- Characters animate on twos (12 fps) for poses, mouths and line boil; camera,
  wipes and effects run on ones. Line width stays constant in screen space.
  Moods change through a squash-and-blink take, never a snap.
- Transitions come from the action (a chomp to black, a fall, a zoom through an
  eye) or from the next world's signature style. At least two real silences and
  at least four different camera moves.
- Dialogue: synthesize each line, transcribe it back to check the words, drive
  the mouth from the audio's loudness and brightness, and for an interruption
  generate the line longer and cut it.
- Code is good at rules. Realistic people come from an image or video model on
  a pure green background, keyed and composited by code.
</type>

<direction>
- Style: one look for the whole film, described so an expert names it in one
  second. Same character or object looks the same in every shot.
- Composition: the subject fills at least a third of the frame height; 5% safe
  margins; a 60% full frame reads deliberate, a 95% full frame reads generated.
- Colour: one main colour and one or two accents.
- Type, for a frame whose short side is 1080 px: subtitles at least 60 px, any text the viewer must read at
  least 48 px, titles at least 72 px, at most two type sizes per frame. Contrast
  at least 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for graphics, measured on the worst spot of a
  textured background.
- Text dwell: one idea per screen; CJK characters / 9 + 0.5 s, Latin words / 3
  + 0.5 s, never under 1.2 s; hold at least 1 s after a conclusion or a stamp.
- Pacing: a hook in the first 0.5 s, never more than 0.3 s of empty or black; a
  visible change every 2-3 s, densest in the first 3 s; one unmistakable hero
  moment at 60-75% of the runtime; an end frame held 1-1.5 s with living
  micro-motion. Stillness longer than 2 s only as a deliberate hold.
- Banned: a dark or blue-purple gradient background with centred big type;
  everything fading in and out; the same slow push in every shot; identical
  timing on every move; linear motion unless it is mechanical; crossfade as the
  default transition; particle bursts, glows and lens flares unless the style
  calls for them; on-screen text that repeats the narration; invented numbers,
  quotes, names or prices; real brands or logos I did not provide; "made with
  code" badges or watermarks; plus the extra bans in <inputs>.
</direction>

<motion>
- Springs are closed-form step responses. When a value changes target several
  times, sum one spring per change so it stays a pure function of t. One tier
  per kind of element (w = angular frequency, z = damping ratio):
    buttons, toggles, leading edges     w 22-26  z 0.75-0.85
    cards, containers, camera           w 14-16  z 0.70-0.75
    big type, 3D objects, logo lockup   w 9-11   z 0.85-1.0
    mascots, stickers                   w 16-20  z 0.40-0.50
- Arrive with ease-out, leave with ease-in, move the camera with ease-in-out.
  Put peak speed early in a move. Vary durations; when every move takes the
  same 0.4 s the film feels mechanical.
- Every shot: entrance (0.2-0.5 s, starting 0.03-0.1 s before its word or beat)
  -> main action -> secondary motion (2-6 px drift, 1-3% scale, 1-2 degrees on
  1.5-4 s periods, at most two kinds at once) -> exit or transition (0.3-0.7 s).
- Events, not cycles: pin each main action to a beat or a word and let it
  happen once. Loops only for blinks, sparks and backgrounds.
- Camera vocabulary: hold; slow push 0.6-1.5 s by 10-40%; pull back 0.8-2 s;
  lateral glide 0.5-0.9 s; punch 0.25-0.35 s by 18-32%; whip 0.26-0.30 s; slam
  0.17 s with scale 1.55 -> 0.94 -> 1.0; cut. Accents on at most a third of the
  shots, never three push-ins in a row. Interpolate zoom in log space. Shake
  lasts 0.15-0.25 s and decays, at most two or three times per film, and moves
  the background too.
- Transitions: at least three types, never the same one three times in a row.
  Use the material's own move (paper tears or falls, a board is erased, a screen
  glitches, a page turns, one shape becomes the next) or the next style's
  signature. A full-screen wipe overscales past the corners and takes about
  0.3 s. Outgoing text leaves before incoming text takes the same space. No more
  than three flashes in any one second.
</motion>

<sound>
- One cue sheet (beats, hits, narration lines) is the single source of timing
  for picture, sound and subtitles. Place each sound by its sync point at the
  visual event time: a hit on its transient, a whoosh on its pass-by peak, a
  riser on its end. Never offset sounds by hand.
- If I supply music, measure its beat grid and downbeats by fitting a grid to
  the onsets (a single tempo estimate can be off by 2%), start on a downbeat and
  put the hero moment on the drop. Otherwise synthesize music in code (kick with
  a pitch drop, bass, detuned chords with reverb, a melody), or use only CC0 or
  licensed samples recorded in CREDITS.md.
- Use silence as punctuation at least once; when the music cuts, cut its reverb
  tail as well.
- Narration first: synthesize or load it, measure every line, then time the
  picture to it. Change the scene 0.1-0.2 s before a line starts. Never speed the
  voice up to fit. Duck the music 8-10 dB under the voice.
- Master to -14 LUFS integrated, true peak at most -1 dBTP, 48 kHz stereo AAC.
  You cannot listen, so check the loudness numbers and look at a waveform and a
  spectrogram image for clipping, gaps and constant low rumble.
</sound>

<build>
1. One self-contained index.html, unless I named a framework. Expose
   window.DURATION, window.ready (true only after fonts and assets load) and
   window.render(t) with t in seconds.
2. Every pixel is a pure function of t. No requestAnimationFrame loops,
   setTimeout, CSS transitions or animations, Date.now, physics that accumulates
   between frames, or unseeded Math.random. Use a seeded hash keyed by object
   index; for a hand-drawn boil quantize jitter with floor(t * 12). Pre-compute
   simulations or solve them in closed form. Reset all canvas state at the start
   of every frame.
3. Fonts and images are local files; wait for document.fonts.ready. Bundle a CJK
   font for Chinese text (Noto Sans SC or Source Han Sans) with a fallback
   stack. No network requests while rendering.
4. Never put will-change on anything the camera scales. Text that swaps inside a
   morphing shape gets its own mask and timing.
5. Renderer: Playwright opens the page at the film size with deviceScaleFactor
   1, calls render(t) for each frame, captures JPEG quality 95 or the canvas
   pixels, and pipes them to ffmpeg: libx264, yuv420p, crf 18, +faststart, BT.709
   tags. Run one browser per worker for parallel rendering and write frames
   atomically so a render can resume. For fast motion add motion blur: 4
   sub-frames inside a 180-degree shutter, blended with ffmpeg tmix, never
   straddling a hard cut.
6. Mux the mastered audio. Render each aspect ratio from the same timeline with
   its own layout; never crop 16:9 into 9:16.
</build>

<review>
- Before motion: 3-10 key-frame stills, each worthy of a poster.
- After the full render:
  - Check the file: exact duration, resolution, fps, audio present, loudness,
    no black frames.
  - Make a contact sheet every 0.5 s, the same sheet 360 px wide for a phone, and
    a strip of 12 consecutive frames around each fast move. Look at all of them.
  - Determinism: render a frame, render a different frame, render the first
    again; the two images must be identical.
  - Score 1-10 as a picky motion director, not a proud author: opening hook,
    phone readability, motion craft, a new change every 2-4 s, composition,
    style fidelity, sound and sync, faithfulness to my facts and brief.
    6 = template level, 8 = high-end agency, 9 = award shortlist.
  - List the three worst problems with timestamps. Fix the lowest score first,
    re-render only those seconds, and repeat until every score is 8 or more, at
    most three rounds; report anything still below 8. Log each round in
    qa/review_log.md.
  - If you can start a subagent, give one with a clean context only the finished
    video and the sheets, not the code, and have it report problems with
    timestamps.
</review>

<deliver>
out/final.mp4 (plus one file per aspect ratio, and a version without subtitles
or music where relevant), the source with a one-command build, brief.md,
CREDITS.md with every asset and its licence, and a short report: the concept in
two sentences; a beat sheet with timecodes; how each signature feature was
realised; check numbers (duration, resolution, fps, loudness, true peak, render
time); the final scores; your honest three biggest weaknesses; which parameters
change the intensity; and any fact you could not verify.
</deliver>
